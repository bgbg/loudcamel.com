How to Write an Academic Press Release That Gets Picked Up

By Boris Gorelik · July 20, 2026

The Communication Gap Between Labs and Newsrooms

Every breakthrough study deserves public visibility, yet most institutional announcements fall flat. Journalists receive hundreds of pitches a day, and if they have to read through four pages of dense methodological jargon just to find the point of your study, they will hit delete.

A successful academic press release is not a dumbed-down version of your paper. It is an exercise in translation.

Public trust in science relies heavily on clear context, honest limitations, and plain-language findings. Your job is to hand journalists a structured, high-signal narrative that makes the societal value of your research instantly undeniable.

The Inverse Pyramid Structural Framework

Journalists write using the inverse pyramid style: leading with the most critical real-world outcome and layering in details later. Flip your standard academic format upside down when writing for the media.

1.Lead with the immediate human impact:The Hook (Paragraph 1).

State exactly what was discovered and why it matters today. Align your findings with global priorities, such as the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), which act as critical relevance markers for modern media outlets.

2.Provide the concrete peer perspective:The Narrative Context (Paragraph 2-3).

Explain what the world looked like before your study, and how your discovery changes that picture. Include an authentic, pre-written quote from the lead investigator explaining the team’s motivation in plain language.

3.Detail the methodology with extreme simplicity:The Grounding (Paragraph 4).

Briefly state how the study was conducted (e.g., a 5-year study tracking 10,000 data points) to establish immediate scientific authority, while explicitly stating any boundaries or uncertainties to maintain absolute transparency.

3 Traps to Avoid in Science Communication

  • The Metaphor Trap: While simple analogies help explain complex systems, avoid over-simplifying to the point where your findings sound sensationalized or misleading.
  • Hiding the Funding: Modern research communication demands radical transparency. Always clearly state who funded the project and note any institutional collaborations right at the bottom of the release.
  • The PDF Bottleneck: Never send your press release as an attached, locked PDF. Journalists need to copy, paste, and extract text quickly. Provide your release as clean, responsive text on an open-access project page with high-resolution visual downloads readily available.

Make your research unmissable. When a press release links directly to open-access preprint versions and replication code repositories, it experiences significantly higher pick-up rates from tech-forward journalists and industry newsletters.
